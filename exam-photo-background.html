Every Indian competitive exam portal — SSC, UPSC, IBPS, RRB, NTA, and CUET — has its own strict photo requirements, and they do not all agree on the same dimensions, file size, or even aspect ratio. A photo that works perfectly for SSC CGL can get rejected by UPSC on the same day. The most common reason for application rejection is not a wrong answer on the exam — it is a photo that does not meet the portal's specifications. This guide lists the exact pixel dimensions, maximum file size limits, background color rules, and acceptable formats for every major Indian exam as of September 2026, so you can prepare your photograph once and apply it everywhere without last-minute panic.

Why Exam Photos Get Rejected

Indian exam portals run automated validation on uploaded photographs before accepting an application. If your photo fails even one check, the portal blocks the upload and you lose time re-taking the photo close to the deadline. Here are the most common reasons photos get rejected:

  • Wrong dimensions: Each exam specifies exact pixel dimensions or a physical size in centimetres. UPSC wants 350 x 450 pixels; IBPS wants 450 x 350 pixels. Mixing these up — submitting a portrait-oriented photo to a landscape-oriented portal — is the single most common cause of rejection.
  • File too large: Most portals cap photo file size at 20 KB to 50 KB. A photo straight from your phone camera can easily be 2 MB to 5 MB. Without compression, the upload will fail every time.
  • Non-white background: SSC, UPSC, IBPS, RRB, and NTA all require a white or light background. Shadowy grey walls, blue studio backdrops, or patterned backgrounds will trigger rejection. The background must be a uniform, clean white.
  • Blurry or low-quality photo: If the face is not sharp, the portal may reject the image or your photo will not match your face at the exam centre. Poor lighting, camera shake, and low-resolution phone cameras all contribute to blurry results.
  • Face not centred: The exam photo must show your face front-on, centred in the frame, with a clear view of both ears and the full forehead. Photos taken at an angle, cropped too tightly, or with excessive headroom fail the centring check.
  • Old or modified photos: Some portals reject photos that appear digitally edited beyond background replacement. Over-smoothing skin, heavy filters, or altering facial features can cause rejection during physical verification at the exam centre.
  • Wearing glasses or headgear without declaration: If you wear spectacles or a religious head covering, the portal may require a separate declaration. Forgetting to attach it can cause issues at verification even if the photo itself is accepted.

The dimensions listed above represent the minimum pixel counts derived from the physical size requirements — uploading a higher-resolution image that maintains the correct aspect ratio is generally accepted, but uploading a lower-resolution image is not.

One critical detail many applicants miss is the difference in orientation. SSC, UPSC, and RRB use a portrait orientation (taller than wide), while IBPS and CUET use a landscape orientation (wider than tall). This means you cannot use the same photo for IBPS and UPSC without re-cropping. Plan ahead and prepare two versions if you are applying to exams from both groups.

File size limits also vary significantly. UPSC has the strictest cap at just 20 KB, which means you will need to compress your photo aggressively. A typical phone photo at 350 x 450 pixels with a white background should compress easily to under 20 KB, but if your original image is high-resolution or has unnecessary colour data, you may need an extra compression step. Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Submitting a selfie with a busy or coloured background instead of using a plain white background. Even if the photo looks fine on your phone screen, the exam portal will reject it.
  • Using the same pixel dimensions for every exam. SSC wants portrait (350 x 450 px) but IBPS wants landscape (450 x 350 px). Always check the specific exam's orientation requirement.
  • Ignoring the file size limit. UPSC's 20 KB cap is very tight. A photo that works for IBPS at 45 KB will fail on UPSC. Always verify the file size after downloading.
  • Using an old photo that no longer looks like you. The photo on your exam application must match your face at the time of the exam. If you have changed your hairstyle, grown a beard, or your appearance has changed significantly, retake the photo.
  • Cropping the photo too tightly around your face. Most exam portals require your full face, neck, and the top of your shoulders to be visible. A head-only crop will not meet the requirement.
  • Adding a border or frame around the photo. Exam portals do not accept photos with decorative borders, rounded corners, or white frames drawn around the image.
  • Uploading a PNG file when the portal only accepts JPEG. Every major Indian exam portal requires JPEG format. Convert before uploading.
  • Waiting until the last day to prepare the photo. If your photo is rejected, you need time to retake it, re-edit, and re-upload. Prepare your photo at least a week before the application deadline.
  • Using a photo with visible shadows on the background. Even a white wall with a shadow from your body can be flagged. Use even lighting or use BgClear to replace the background with a clean, uniform white.
  • Forgetting to check the photo after resizing. Always open the downloaded JPEG and confirm it looks correct — face centred, background white, dimensions right, file size within limits — before submitting.

Frequently Asked Questions

What background color is required for SSC/UPSC photos?

Both SSC and UPSC require a plain white background for the photograph. The photo must have a clean, uniform white backdrop with no patterns, shadows, or gradients. A light grey background is sometimes accepted on IBPS portals, but for SSC and UPSC you should always use pure white to be safe.

What file size should my exam photo be?

Most Indian competitive exams require the photo file to be between 20 KB and 50 KB. The UPSC Civil Services exam has a stricter upper limit of 20 KB. NTA NEET accepts files in the 10 KB to 200 KB range. Always compress your photo to meet the specific limit for the exam you are applying to, and verify the exact size on the official exam portal before submission.

Can I use BgClear to resize my photo to exam specifications?

Yes. BgClear can remove the background from your photo and replace it with a plain white background. After that, you can resize the image to the exact pixel dimensions required by your exam. BgClear runs entirely in your browser so your photo never leaves your device, and you can download the result as a JPEG file ready for upload.

What happens if my photo doesn't meet the requirements?

If your uploaded photo does not match the exam's specifications — wrong dimensions, wrong file size, non-white background, or poor quality — the application portal will typically reject the upload or show an error message. In some cases, the application may be accepted but the photo can be rejected during the admit card or verification stage, which could prevent you from sitting for the exam. Always double-check the specifications on the official portal and test your photo before the deadline.
