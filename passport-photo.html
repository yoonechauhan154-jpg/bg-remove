1. Passport Photo Requirements in India

Indian passport photos follow a simple set of expectations that most applicants get wrong in the same two places: background and glare. Officials want a clear, recent photo taken against a plain light background — white or off-white is the accepted standard — with your face fully visible, centered, and looking straight at the camera. Neutral expression, both ears visible if your hair allows, no heavy shadows, no tinted lenses, and no smile with teeth showing. It sounds fussy, but every rule exists because photo-booth photos are checked by both software and human eyes.

Here's the part people miss: the photo must look like it was taken in a controlled setting, not like a screenshot of a birthday party. That's precisely why removing the background and replacing it with clean white works so well — it instantly kills the "living room photo" look, removes patterned curtains and wall clocks from the equation, and makes the image read as formal. The face stays exactly the same; the context changes entirely.

One more Indian-specific note: keep your ears visible and your forehead uncovered. Bangs and caps are the top two reasons photos get rejected, and neither is something a background remover can fix. Remove the hair or hat before you take the photo, not after.

2. How to Get White Background for ID Photos

The workflow is short enough to memorize. Take a well-lit photo of yourself against any reasonably plain background — even a painted wall works. Upload it to BgClear, and the AI will separate you from whatever is behind you. Then replace that backdrop with plain white and download the result. If the source photo is sharp and evenly lit, the final image will look like it came out of a studio.

Three practical details make the difference between "good enough for WhatsApp" and "good enough for a visa office":

  • Light your face, not the wall. Stand facing a window so the light hits you straight on. Backlighting turns your shoulders into a glowing blur and makes the cutout edge messy.
  • Shoot at eye level, arms-length or on a tripod. Shooting from below gives you a double chin and a tilted horizon; shooting from above crops your forehead. Either one can get a photo bounced.
  • Keep the resolution high. Passport photos get inspected close-up. A sharp 1500px+ source crops down beautifully; a 400px selfie does not.

After you've got the white background, resize to the exact dimensions the application asks for. Most people forget this step and submit a huge or oddly proportioned file — the background is right, but the size is wrong, and it gets rejected anyway.

4. Tips for Perfect Passport Photos

Having reviewed and submitted a shameful number of document photos over the years, here are the rules I actually live by:

  • Soft, even light on your face. A window at noon is fine; a direct flash creates hot spots on skin and glare on glasses, both of which cause rejections.
  • Head centered, looking straight ahead. Officials compare your photo to your face at the counter. If you're tilted or smirking, it introduces doubt.
  • Nothing covering your face. Caps, hats, tinted glasses, heavy filters — all grounds for rejection. Religious headwear is generally allowed if your full face stays visible.
  • Check edges after removal. After you drop in the white background, zoom in around your hairline and shoulders. A clean, even edge is what makes the photo look professionally made.
  • Verify before submitting. Check size, resolution, background color, and file format against the requirements, then check again. The application form won't catch mistakes for you.

And one honest caveat: a background remover fixes the background — it does not fix the photo. If the lighting is harsh or the image is blurry, no amount of white backdrop will rescue it. Start from a photo you'd be happy to show an official, then let BgClear handle the backdrop.

5. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a selfie for a passport photo?

Technically yes, if the photo meets the requirements: front-facing, even light, neutral expression, plain background, correct size. In practice, a selfie taken at arm's length tends to have distortion and shadows near the edges. A photo taken by someone else, or with a phone propped up, usually passes more easily.

Does the white background need to be pure white?

For most applications, white or off-white is fine — the rule is "plain and light," not "RGB 255,255,255 exactly." Pure white is the safest choice and the easiest to defend if a photo is questioned.

Will my passport photo be rejected if I use a background remover?

The rejection criteria are about the final image, not the tool used to make it. As long as the background is plain and light, the face is clear and correctly sized, and nothing looks digitally pasted or distorted, you're fine. Don't over-sharpen or apply filters that make the cutout look artificial.

Can BgClear resize my photo to passport dimensions?

BgClear focuses on removing the background and giving you a clean transparent PNG. Resizing and cropping to the exact passport dimensions you'll do in your phone's photo editor or any free resize tool — it's a two-minute job once the background is clean.

What background color do different visa photos need?

Visa photo rules vary by country — the US wants white, Malaysia wants light blue, Indonesia wants red. If you are applying for a visa, check our visa photo background color guide for a country-by-country breakdown before you take your photo.

What about the 2×2 inch US photo size in pixels?

2×2 inches at 300 DPI works out to 600×600 pixels, which is the commonly accepted digital spec for US visa photos. Check the current guidance on the official site before submitting, since specifications do change.
