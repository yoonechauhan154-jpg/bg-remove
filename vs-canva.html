3. Privacy & Image Processing

This is where the comparison gets interesting, and it's the reason BgClear exists at all.

Canva is a cloud-based application. When you upload a photo to Canva — whether for background removal, editing, or adding it to a design — that image is transmitted to Canva's servers. It lives on their infrastructure, is processed by their AI models in their data centers, and is subject to their privacy policy. Canva says they use uploaded content to provide and improve their services. For most personal use, this is fine. But for certain workflows, it's a dealbreaker.

BgClear never uploads your image anywhere. The AI model runs entirely in your browser using WebAssembly and client-side JavaScript. Your photo goes from your device to the model to your download — it never touches a server. There is no cloud storage, no data retention, no third-party processing.

This matters for several concrete use cases:

  • Client work: If you're a photographer or designer cutting out images for a client, sending their photos to Canva's servers may violate your NDA or client agreement.
  • Sensitive products: Unreleased product photos, confidential documents, or proprietary imagery shouldn't be uploaded to third-party services.
  • Personal photos: Some people simply don't want their personal images stored on a company's servers, regardless of the privacy policy.
  • Regulatory compliance: Industries with strict data handling requirements (healthcare, legal, government) may prohibit uploading images to cloud services.

For more detail on how browser-based processing works and why it matters, see our guide on no-upload background removers.

6. The Privacy Angle

Let's talk about the part most comparison pages skip.

Canva stores your images in their cloud. When you upload a photo to Canva, it goes to their servers, gets processed by their AI, and is stored according to their data retention policies. Canva's privacy policy gives them broad rights to use uploaded content for service improvement. For a social media graphic or a personal project, this is probably fine. But it's worth understanding what you're agreeing to.

For certain types of work, cloud-based processing creates real problems:

  • Freelance photographers processing client portraits can't always send those images to third-party servers without violating their contracts.
  • E-commerce sellers with unreleased product photos risk leaks if those images sit on a cloud server before launch.
  • Real estate agents handling photos of client properties have confidentiality obligations.
  • Anyone processing personal photos — medical images, family photos, legal evidence — may have good reasons to keep them off corporate servers.

BgClear's no-upload model eliminates this entirely. Your image is processed in your browser, on your device, using your hardware. Nothing is sent anywhere. For the privacy-conscious user, this isn't a minor feature — it's the entire reason to choose a browser-based tool over a cloud service.
