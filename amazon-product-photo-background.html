Amazon Product Photo Background — White BG Rules & Requirements (2026)

🔒 Your photo never leaves your device

Selling on Amazon? Your product photo background can make or break a listing. Amazon enforces strict image requirements for all sellers — and the most common reason listings get suppressed is a non-compliant main image background. Whether you're launching your first SKU or managing a catalog of hundreds, this guide covers every Amazon product photo background rule you need to know in 2026, from the exact RGB values to file specs and the 85% frame rule. Amazon's Main Image Requirements (Strict Rules)

Amazon's main product image — the first image buyers see in search results and on your product detail page — must follow a specific set of rules. These are non-negotiable, and Amazon's automated systems enforce them before your listing goes live. A single violation can result in your image being rejected, your listing suppressed, or your product removed from search results entirely.

Here is the complete breakdown of every requirement Amazon enforces for the main image:

RequirementRule
Background colorPure white — RGB 255, 255, 255 (hex #FFFFFF). No gradients, no shadows, no off-white shades.
Product fillProduct must occupy at least 85% of the total image frame.
Text & overlaysNo text, logos, watermarks, badges, graphics, or promotional overlays of any kind.
File formatJPEG (.jpg) preferred. TIFF (.tif) and PNG are also accepted. JPEG offers the best quality-to-size ratio.
File sizeMaximum 10 MB per image.
Color modeRGB. CMYK files will be rejected.
ResolutionMinimum 500 x 500 px. Recommended: 2000 x 2000 px or larger for zoom functionality.
Props & accessoriesNo props, no other items, no packaging — just the product alone on white.
FocusThe product itself, not the background, must be the clear focal point of the image.

Why these rules exist: Amazon wants a uniform, clean shopping experience. When every product on the platform uses a consistent pure-white main image, buyers can compare products without visual noise. The 85% fill rule ensures thumbnails in search results are large enough to be useful. And the no-text rule prevents sellers from cramming promotional messages into what should be a product photograph.

Amazon's Additional Image Requirements

While the main image has strict rules, Amazon's secondary gallery images (images 2 through 8) are much more flexible. These extra slots are your opportunity to show the product from different angles, in real-world settings, and with supporting information. Here's what's allowed and what's not:

What You Can Do With Secondary Images

  • Different backgrounds: Lifestyle shots, colored backgrounds, gradient backgrounds, or contextual scenes are all permitted.
  • Props and accessories: Show the product in use — a coffee mug on a desk, a backpack on a person, headphones on a model.
  • Infographics: Add text overlays, dimension callouts, feature highlights, comparison charts, and instructional graphics to explain your product's value.
  • Multiple angles: Show the front, back, sides, bottom, and any unique features that help buyers understand what they're purchasing.
  • Scale references: Include a hand, a coin, or a ruler to communicate product size intuitively.

What's Still Not Allowed

  • Competitor branding: Don't reference or show competitor products, logos, or trademarks.
  • Pricing information: Amazon prohibits displaying prices, discount percentages, or "sale" badges in product images.
  • Accurate representation: Images must still accurately represent the actual product. Misleading photos that don't match the item being sold can lead to returns and policy violations.
  • Restricted content: No nudity, violence, or prohibited items per Amazon's general content policies.

A strong Amazon listing typically uses the main image for a clean, compliant white-background shot, then uses images 2–8 for a mix of lifestyle photos, infographics, and close-up details that answer common buyer questions before they're even asked.

How to Check If Your Background Is Truly White

The most common mistake Amazon sellers make is assuming their background is white when it isn't quite. A photo that looks white on screen can easily have an RGB value of 248, 248, 248 or 250, 250, 250 — and Amazon's system will reject it. Here's how to verify your image before uploading:

1

Open in a Photo Editor

Use any image editor — Photoshop, GIMP, Photopea (free online), or even the built-in editors on Mac and Windows.

2

Use the Eyedropper Tool

Click on the background area of your image with the eyedropper or color picker tool to sample the RGB values.

3

Check for RGB 255,255,255

The values must read exactly 255, 255, 255. Anything less — even 250, 250, 250 — is considered off-white and can be flagged.

The near-white trap: Many photo editors and cameras produce backgrounds that appear white but have subtle gradients or slight color casts. Light grey (250, 250, 250) is the most common offender — it's virtually indistinguishable from white to the human eye but fails Amazon's automated checks. This is precisely why using a background remover with an explicit white fill option is more reliable than trying to manually adjust brightness or exposure in post-production.

Common Amazon Photo Rejection Reasons

Understanding why images get rejected helps you avoid the same mistakes. Based on Amazon Seller Central feedback and common community reports, these are the most frequent reasons product photos fail compliance checks:

  • Background not pure white (most common): The background reads as 248/250/252 instead of exactly 255/255/255. This is by far the number one reason listings get flagged. Even a single pixel off-white in the corners can trigger a rejection. Use BgClear's white fill to guarantee exact values.
  • Product doesn't fill 85% of frame: If the product appears small relative to the overall image dimensions — too much white space around the edges — Amazon rejects the image. Crop tightly around the product before exporting.
  • Watermarks or text on image: Any text overlay, logo, watermark, badge, or promotional graphic on the main image violates Amazon's policy. The main image must be a clean product photograph with no additions.
  • Low resolution or blurry: Images below 500 x 500 pixels or photos that are noticeably out of focus will be rejected. Aim for 2000 x 2000 pixels or larger, and ensure the product is sharp and well-lit.
  • Outdated or inaccurate representation: If the photo doesn't match the actual product being sold — wrong color, wrong model, missing components — Amazon can flag the listing for misleading content. Always use current, accurate product photos.
  • CMYK color mode: Some designers export images in CMYK mode intended for print. Amazon requires RGB. Check your export settings before uploading.
  • Shadows or gradients on background: Drop shadows, gradient backgrounds, or any non-uniform white area counts as a non-compliant background. The entire background must be flat, uniform #FFFFFF.

What happens when your image is rejected? Amazon will typically flag the issue in Seller Central under your listing's image status. You'll have an opportunity to upload a corrected image. However, if you don't address the issue promptly, the listing can be suppressed — meaning it disappears from search results entirely, cutting off your organic traffic until the image is fixed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What RGB values does Amazon require for product photo backgrounds?

Amazon requires a pure white background with RGB values of 255, 255, 255 (hex #FFFFFF) for the main product image. This is not a suggestion — Amazon's automated systems check for the exact RGB value. A background of 250, 250, 250 or any other near-white shade can be flagged as non-compliant. Using a background remover with an explicit white fill option, like BgClear, is the most reliable way to get exact values.

What does the 85% frame rule mean?

The 85% frame rule means your product must fill at least 85% of the total image area. If your product photo has too much empty white space around the item, Amazon considers it non-compliant. This ensures thumbnails and search results show the product prominently rather than a tiny item lost in white space. To meet this rule, crop tightly around your product after removing the background, ensuring it dominates the frame with only a small margin of white around the edges.

Can I use BgClear to prepare Amazon product photos?

Yes. BgClear removes the background from your product photo entirely in the browser, then you can select the white background option to get a pure #FFFFFF fill. The result is an RGB 255, 255, 255 background that meets Amazon's requirements. Export as JPEG and upload to Seller Central. What file format should my Amazon product photos be?

Amazon accepts JPEG (.jpg), TIFF (.tif), and PNG formats for product images, but JPEG is the preferred format. JPEG files offer the best balance of quality and file size for Amazon listings. Keep file size under 10 MB and ensure the file is in RGB color mode (not CMYK). For best results, export at the highest JPEG quality setting while staying within the 10 MB limit.

How do I resize my photo to meet Amazon's requirements?

Amazon requires a minimum resolution of 500 x 500 pixels, though 2000 x 2000 pixels or larger is strongly recommended to enable the zoom feature on product pages. After removing the background, use any photo editor to resize the image. Most editors let you set the dimensions in pixels — enter 2000 x 2000 (or larger) while maintaining the aspect ratio. Then crop tightly around the product to ensure it fills at least 85% of the frame.
