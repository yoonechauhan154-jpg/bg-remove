Driving Licence Photo Requirements

The Government of India, through the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (MoRTH) and the centralised Parivahan portal, has standardised most driving licence photo requirements. While individual states may have minor variations, the core specifications are consistent nationwide. Here is what you need to know:

Background Color

A plain, solid white background is mandatory for driving licence photographs in India. This is the single most important requirement. Your photo must be taken or edited against a pure white backdrop — no grey tones, no shadows, no gradients, and no patterned backgrounds. The white background ensures your face is clearly visible and the photo meets the biometric standards expected by RTO officials and the automated systems used on the Parivahan portal.

If your original photo was taken against a coloured wall, a busy background, or any non-white surface, you will need to replace the background before uploading. This is where a tool like BgClear becomes essential — it removes the existing background in your browser and lets you export the photo with a clean white background in seconds.

Photo Dimensions

The standard driving licence photo size in India is 3.5 cm × 4.5 cm. When converted to digital pixels at standard resolution, this is approximately 350 × 450 pixels. Some portals may specify slightly different pixel dimensions, so always check the exact requirements on your state's transport portal or the Parivahan Sarathi website before uploading.

The photo must show your face clearly from the front, with adequate spacing above the head and on both sides. The image should include your full face, from the top of your head to the bottom of your chin, with both ears visible where possible. Hair should not cover the face, and the expression should be neutral with eyes open and looking straight at the camera.

File Format

JPEG is the standard format accepted by virtually all RTO portals and the Parivahan Sarathi system. PNG files are sometimes accepted but JPEG is the safest choice. Make sure the image is saved as a standard JPEG with no unusual compression artifacts that could make your photo look blurry or distorted.

File Size

The maximum file size for a driving licence photo varies by state and portal, but typically falls in the range of 10 KB to 50 KB. Some portals are stricter and accept only files up to 20 KB, while others allow up to 50 KB. The key is to keep the file as small as possible while maintaining acceptable image quality. Important Note on State Variations

While the central Parivahan portal sets the baseline, individual states may have slightly different requirements. Some states specify exact pixel dimensions, different maximum file sizes, or additional rules about photo composition. Always check your specific state's transport department website or the Parivahan Sarathi portal for the most up-to-date requirements before submitting your application. Tips for a Good Driving Licence Photo

A technically correct photo is only half the battle — the photo also needs to look professional and meet the RTO's guidelines for appearance. Follow these tips to make sure your driving licence photo is accepted on the first attempt:

  • No glasses glare. If you normally wear prescription glasses, remove them for the photo. Glare on the lenses is one of the top reasons photos get rejected at RTO counters. If you must wear glasses, make sure there is absolutely no glare or reflection on the lenses.
  • Neutral facial expression. Keep a calm, neutral expression with your mouth closed. Do not smile broadly, frown, or make any exaggerated expressions. The photo is for identification purposes and your face should appear natural and relaxed.
  • No hats or headwear. Headwear is generally not allowed in driving licence photos. The exception is for religious reasons — if you wear a turban, hijab, or other religious head covering, you may wear it, but your full face must still be clearly visible from the forehead to the chin.
  • Good, even lighting. Take the photo in a well-lit area with even lighting on your face. Avoid harsh shadows on one side of the face, strong backlighting, or flash reflections. Natural daylight from a window works well, or use a simple lamp setup that illuminates your face evenly from the front.
  • Face the camera straight on. Your face should be centred in the frame, facing the camera directly. Do not tilt your head, look to the side, or angle your body. Both eyes should be clearly visible and looking straight ahead.
  • Recent photo. The photo should be recent and clearly represent your current appearance. Old photos that no longer resemble you can cause issues at the RTO during verification.
  • Proper cropping. Make sure the photo shows your head and upper shoulders. There should be a small amount of space above the top of your head, and the bottom of the photo should end at or just below your shoulders. Do not crop too tightly or too loosely.
